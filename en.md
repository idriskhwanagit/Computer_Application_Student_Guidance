# Lesson 1 — The computer, and choosing a laptop

> **Today you will learn to** name the parts of a computer, choose a suitable laptop, switch a
> computer on and off properly, and use the mouse and the keyboard.

## 1. What is a computer?

A computer is a machine you do your work with: writing, reading, sending e-mail, watching
pictures and videos, and giving presentations.

There are two common kinds:

- **Desktop computer:** its screen, keyboard and mouse are separate, and it always stays plugged in.
- **Laptop:** all its parts are in one machine; it has a battery and you carry it with you.

In this course we work mostly on laptops.

![A ThinkPad laptop: the screen at the top; the keyboard and the touchpad below](img/photos-laptop.webp)

## 2. Three parts inside

These three parts are inside the laptop and you do not see them, but when you buy a laptop they
matter most.

### Processor (CPU) — the computer's brain

The processor does every job. How fast a computer is depends mostly on this part.

![An Intel Core i7-8550U processor on a laptop's motherboard](img/photos-processor.webp)

### RAM — the work desk

It is like a desk you put today's work on. The bigger it is, the more programs you can keep open
at once. When you shut the computer down, the RAM is emptied.

![Laptop RAM: 8 GB](img/photos-ram.webp)

### Storage (SSD) — the cupboard for your files

Your files and programs stay here, even while the computer is off. An **SSD** is much faster
than an old **hard disk (HDD)**, so choose an SSD in a new laptop.

![An SSD: small and fast storage](img/photos-ssd.webp)

> **In short:** processor = brain · RAM = work desk · SSD = cupboard

## 3. Parts outside

- **Screen:** what the computer shows you.
- **Keyboard:** for typing. We look at it closely in part 7.
- **Touchpad:** the laptop's built-in mouse. Move your finger on it and the mouse pointer (cursor) moves on the screen.
- **Mouse:** does the same job as the touchpad, but many people find it easier.

![A touchpad: its left and right buttons are at the bottom](img/photos-touchpad.webp)

![A mouse: the left button, the right button and the scroll wheel in the middle](img/photos-mouse.webp){small}

## 4. Choosing a laptop for Windows 11 and Office 2024

If you have no laptop yet, or want to change yours, take this table with you to the shop.

| Part | What to buy |
|---|---|
| Laptop | A ThinkPad |
| Processor | Core i5 or Core i7, 8th generation or newer |
| RAM | 8 GB or 16 GB |
| Storage | An SSD of 256 GB or 512 GB |

### How do you find the processor's generation?

In the name of an Intel processor, the number after i3, i5 or i7 shows the generation:

- If the number starts with **1**, its **first two** digits are the generation: 1135 → 11th generation
- Otherwise its **first** digit is the generation: 8250 → 8th generation

| Processor name | Generation | Windows 11 |
|---|---|---|
| i5-7200U | 7 | ✗ will not run it |
| i5-8250U | 8 | ✓ |
| i5-10210U | 10 | ✓ |
| i5-1135G7 | 11 | ✓ |
| Core Ultra 5 | Intel's new name | ✓ |

> ### Careful
> An old second-hand laptop made before 2018 often cannot run Windows 11, however cheap it is.

### What to do in the shop

1. Ask them to open **Settings → System → About**.
2. Check these three: the processor ((1)), the RAM ((2)) and the storage ((3)).
3. If you are not sure, use the **PC Health Check** app. It tells you whether that laptop can run Windows 11.

## 5. Switching on and off

### Switching on

1. If the battery is low, plug in the charger.
2. Press the power button once and let go.
3. Wait for the sign-in screen, then type your PIN or password and press [[Enter]].

### Shutting down properly

1. Click the **Start** button, on the bar at the bottom of the screen.
2. Click **Power**.
3. Choose **Shut down** and wait until the screen goes completely dark.

| Choice | What it does | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep** | The computer sleeps; your work stays open | A short break |
| **Shut down** | The computer switches off completely | The end of the day |
| **Restart** | It switches off and back on by itself | When the computer is slow or has a problem |

> ### Never
> Do not hold the power button down for several seconds to switch off — unless the computer has frozen
> completely and nothing works. Anything open and not saved is lost.

## 6. The mouse and the touchpad

The mouse pointer (the cursor) is a small arrow on the screen. Move the mouse, or your finger on
the touchpad, and the cursor moves with it.

| Action | With a mouse | With a touchpad | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Click** | The left button once | Tap once with one finger | Selects something |
| **Double-click** | The left button twice, quickly | Tap twice, quickly | Opens a file or a program |
| **Right-click** | The right button once | Tap with two fingers together | Opens a menu |
| **Drag** | Keep your finger on the left button and move | Tap twice and keep your finger down the second time, then move | Moves something |
| **Scroll** | Turn the scroll wheel in the middle | Move two fingers up or down | Moves the page down and up |

Now try it here. Each time you do one, a green ✓ appears.

# Lesson 2 — Windows and files

> **Today you will learn to** know the taskbar and the Start menu, handle windows, make folders,
> copy and paste files, and use a flash drive.

## 1. The desktop and the taskbar

When the computer starts, the first thing you see is the **desktop**: the face of the screen. At
the bottom of the screen there is a bar called the **taskbar**.

![The taskbar](img/screens-lesson2-taskbar.webp)

| Number | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| ((1)) | The Start button | Opens the Start menu |
| ((2)) | Search | Finds a program, a file or a setting by its name |
| ((3)) | Programs | The programs that are open or pinned; one click opens them |
| ((4)) | Keyboard language | Shows the language you are typing in now |
| ((5)) | Wi-Fi, sound and battery | The state of the internet, the sound and the battery |
| ((6)) | Clock and date | Today's time and date |

> In this picture the bar is on the left. On most laptops the Start button and the programs are in
> the middle, but they do the same things.

## 2. The Start menu

Click the Start button, or press the [[Win]] key.

![The Start menu](img/screens-lesson2-start.webp)

- **Search** ((1)): type the program's name, for example Word, and press [[Enter]].
- **Pinned programs** ((2)): click any of them and it opens.
- **All programs** ((3)): the All button lists every program.
- **Power** ((4)): for Sleep, Shut down and Restart, as you learned in lesson 1.

## 3. Windows

Every program opens in a **window**. In the top right corner of every window there are three
buttons, number ((6)) in the picture of part 4:

| Button | Name | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| — | Minimize | Hides the window in the taskbar; clicking the program there brings it back |
| ☐ | Maximize | The window fills the screen; pressed again, it goes back to its earlier size |
| ✕ | Close | Closes the program |

- **Switching between windows:** click the program in the taskbar, or keep your finger on the
  [[Alt]] key and press [[Tab]].
- **Moving a window:** keep your finger on the left mouse button on the window's top bar, and move.

## 4. File Explorer: files and folders

- **File:** any work you save, such as a letter, a picture or a presentation.
- **Folder:** like a bag you keep your files in, in order. A folder can hold other folders too.

To open File Explorer, click the yellow folder in the taskbar, or press [[Win+E]].

![File Explorer](img/screens-lesson2-explorer.webp)

| Number | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| ((1)) | Where you are | Shows where you are: Documents → Ahmad Ali |
| ((2)) | The New button | Makes a new folder or a new file |
| ((3)) | The action buttons | Cut, copy, paste, rename, share and delete |
| ((4)) | The main folders | Desktop, Downloads, Documents, Pictures, Music and Videos |
| ((5)) | The contents | The files and folders in this place |
| ((6)) | The window buttons | Minimize, maximize and close |

### Making a folder

1. On the left, click **Documents**.
2. Click **New** ((2)) and choose **Folder**. Another way: right-click an empty spot → **New** →
   **Folder**.
3. Type your name and press [[Enter]].

### Renaming, deleting and getting it back

- **Rename:** click the file → press [[F2]] → type the new name → [[Enter]].
- **Delete:** click the file → press [[Delete]]. The file goes to the **Recycle Bin**.
- **Get it back:** on the desktop, double-click the **Recycle Bin** → right-click the file → **Restore**.

## 5. Copy, cut and paste

| Action | With the mouse | With the keyboard | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Copy** | Right-click → Copy | [[Ctrl+C]] | The file stays where it is and a copy is ready |
| **Cut** | Right-click → Cut | [[Ctrl+X]] | The file is ready to be moved |
| **Paste** | Right-click → Paste | [[Ctrl+V]] | The file is put in the new place |

> In Windows 11, Cut, Copy, Paste and Rename appear at the top of the right-click menu as small
> icons, without names. They are the same buttons as ((3)).

To choose several files at once: keep your finger on the [[Ctrl]] key and click the files. To
choose them all: [[Ctrl+A]].

## 6. The flash drive

1. Put the flash drive into a USB port.
2. In File Explorer click **This PC** ((2)). The flash drive appears next to the drives ((1)), with
   a name like **USB Drive**.
3. Copy your file, double-click the flash drive, and paste it.
4. **Removing it safely:** right-click the flash drive → **Eject**, then take it out.

![This PC: the drives and the flash drive appear here](img/screens-lesson2-thispc.webp)

> ### Careful
> Do not pull the flash drive out while it is copying, or the files may be damaged.

  columns and rows, then click. To go to the next cell, press [[Tab]].
- **Picture** ((2)): **Insert** → **Pictures** → **This Device** → choose the picture → **Insert**.
  To make it bigger or smaller, drag a corner.
- **Page numbers** ((3)): **Insert** → **Page Number** → **Bottom of Page** → choose a style. Here
  are **Header** and **Footer** too, for a line at the top or bottom of every page.

## 3. Page setup

# Lesson 7 — PowerPoint

> **Today you will learn to** build a presentation: slides, a design, text and pictures, a simple
> animation, and presenting on the screen.

## 1. A new presentation

Start → **PowerPoint** → **Blank Presentation**.

![PowerPoint](img/screens-lesson7-pp-home.webp)

| Number | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| ((1)) | New Slide | Adds a new slide |
| ((2)) | Layout | Changes the slide's shape: a title, a title and text, two columns… |
| ((3)) | The slides, small | Click one to work on it |
| ((4)) | The slide | Click "Click to add title" and type |

## 2. Design

**Design** → choose one of the designs ((1)). Every slide changes.

![The Design tab](img/screens-lesson7-pp-design.webp)

## 3. Text and pictures

![The Insert tab](img/screens-lesson7-pp-insert.webp)

- **Picture** ((1)): **Insert** → **Pictures** → **This Device** → choose the picture.
- **Text Box** ((2)): click it, then drag on the slide and type.

> **A good slide:** few words, big letters, clear pictures. A slide is not for reading out the whole text.

## 4. A simple animation

1. Select a text or a picture on the slide.
2. **Animations** → choose a simple animation ((1)), such as **Fade** or **Appear**.
3. Press **Preview** ((2)) to see it.

![The Animations tab](img/screens-lesson7-pp-animations.webp)

> Do not overdo animation. One simple animation for the whole presentation is enough.

## 5. Presenting

![The Slide Show tab](img/screens-lesson7-pp-slideshow.webp)

- **From Beginning** ((1)) or [[F5]]: from the first slide.
- **From Current Slide** ((2)) or [[Shift+F5]]: from the slide you are on.
- **Next slide:** click, or press [[→]] or [[Space]].
- **The end:** [[Esc]].
